## Whole-House Installation

A whole-house installation puts one softener on the main water line, so hot and cold water at every fixture is softened. The technician sets the unit near a drain and an electrical outlet, usually in a basement or utility room, and connects the brine tank, drain line and bypass valve. The size depends on your household and on hardness: the City lists 4.7 to 6.6 grains per gallon at its plants, and a [water test](/water-quality-testing/) shows your own number.

## Portable Unit Installation

A portable unit is a small exchange or rental tank, set up on a single line where a full installation is not possible. It suits a rental home or a short stay. The unit has limited capacity, so it needs exchange more often in a larger household, and the technician explains the trade-off at the estimate.

## Bypass Valve Installation

A bypass valve lets you isolate the softener for service while the rest of the house keeps running water. The technician fits one on every installation. It also gives you a way to send untreated water to outdoor taps if you choose.

## Softener Plumbing Connections

The softener connects to the main line with copper, PEX or flexible connectors, and to a drain for the regeneration water. The technician matches the connection type to your existing pipes and the plumbing code. <mark class="data-gap">[NEEDS DATA: Minneapolis permit and plumbing-code requirements for a softener install.]</mark>

## Pressure Regulator & Shut-off Valve Installation

A pressure regulator protects the softener's seals when street pressure runs high, and a shut-off valve lets you stop water to the unit. The technician measures your incoming pressure and adds the parts that your house needs.

## 4-Step Installation Process

Installation follows four steps. First, a technician tests your water and confirms the softener size. Second, the old plumbing is prepared and the unit is placed. Third, the technician connects the bypass valve, drain line and brine tank, then programs the control head. Fourth, every tap is tested and the technician explains salt refills and settings. A standard install typically takes about 2 to 4 hours.
