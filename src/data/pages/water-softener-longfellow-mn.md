## Why Longfellow Residents Need a Water Softener

Longfellow homes drink the City of Minneapolis supply. The City of Minneapolis 2025 record lists average total hardness of 94 mg/L (about 5.5 gpg), within a range of 80 to 113 mg/L, which the U.S. Geological Survey rates moderately hard. The City gives no separate Longfellow figure, so a test at your tap gives the number for your house.

A Macalester College planning report says Longfellow was part of Richfield until Minneapolis annexed it in 1887, and streetcar lines then made it a working-class neighborhood known for bungalows. A MinnPost profile describes 1920s stucco cottages and bungalows on narrow lots, which often have a small utility corner, so the technician measures the space before recommending a unit. At 5.5 gpg, a household of four using about 300 gallons a day removes about 1,650 grains daily, so a 32,000-grain salt-based softener regenerates about every 19 days.

## About Longfellow Water Quality

The City of Minneapolis draws Mississippi River water, treats it at the Fridley and Columbia Heights plants and softens it with lime before distribution. Plant readings in 2025 ran from 88 mg/L in March to 104 mg/L in January. Homes of this age can have older supply lines, and a tap test shows what the water carries after your plumbing.
