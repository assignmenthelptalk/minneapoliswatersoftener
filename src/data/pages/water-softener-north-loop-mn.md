## Why North Loop Residents Need a Water Softener

North Loop buildings drink the City of Minneapolis supply. The City of Minneapolis 2025 record lists average total hardness of 94 mg/L (about 5.5 gpg), within a range of 80 to 113 mg/L, which the U.S. Geological Survey rates moderately hard. The City gives no separate North Loop figure, so a test at your tap gives the number for your unit.

The Minneapolis Warehouse Historic District has a period of significance of 1865 to 1930 and was listed on the National Register in 1989, and many of its warehouses, such as the 1896 Creamette Lofts building, are now residential. Loft and condo owners often share risers and a building water line, so the technician confirms with the building which line a softener can serve, and an under-sink or point-of-use system suits many units. At 5.5 gpg, a household of four using about 300 gallons a day removes about 1,650 grains daily, so a 32,000-grain salt-based softener regenerates about every 19 days.

## About North Loop Water Quality

The City of Minneapolis draws Mississippi River water, treats it at the Fridley and Columbia Heights plants and softens it with lime before distribution. Plant readings in 2025 ran from 4.7 to 6.6 gpg across the year. Converted warehouses can carry older risers, and a tap test shows what reaches your unit.
